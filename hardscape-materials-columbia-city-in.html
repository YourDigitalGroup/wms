Why Material Selection Matters in Indiana's Climate

Indiana's climate puts hardscape materials through real stress: freeze-thaw cycling, ground movement from clay-heavy soils, and wet springs followed by dry summers. Choosing the wrong paver thickness, skipping proper base depth, or using non-rated materials in a fire feature shortens the life of the project significantly.

Warsaw Masonry Supply stocks materials suited to Midwest conditions. When you're selecting pavers or wall block, factors like absorption rate, compressive strength, and freeze-thaw cycling ratings are worth asking about — not just color and texture. Our staff can walk you through the spec sheets on materials you're considering.

I'm building a retaining wall on a sloped lot in Whitley County — how do I know how much block to order?

For a segmental retaining wall, the quantity of block you need depends on the wall's total square footage of face area, not just its linear length. Multiply the length of the wall by the exposed height, then divide by the face area of the individual block unit you choose. Add approximately 10% for cuts and waste. Bring your measurements to Warsaw Masonry Supply and we can help you calculate full pallets, cap units, and drainage aggregate quantities at the same time.

Are your pavers rated to handle Indiana winters? I've had cracking problems before. Concrete pavers suitable for Indiana's climate should carry a low water absorption rate — typically under 5% — and be tested for freeze-thaw durability under ASTM C936 or equivalent standards. Cracking in previous installations is often a base preparation issue rather than a product defect, but underspec'd pavers do fail faster in freeze-thaw conditions. When you visit or contact us, ask for the product spec sheet on any paver you're considering and compare the absorption and strength ratings before purchasing.
